One cable to your board

Donguru sits between your computer and a device with a USB-C port. A USB 2.0 hub, an STM32G0B1 microcontroller and a set of switches give software full control over the device's power, its USB data lines and its sideband pins. Flashing, logging and power cycling become one cable and a script.

A device with a USB-C port usually reaches the bench through several boxes: a cable for USB, an adapter and jumper wires for the serial console, a debug probe, a switchable supply and a meter. Each one is another cable to plug and another thing a test script can't control. Donguru puts all of it in one dongle on the device's own USB-C port, and every part of it answers to software.

It is for everyone who works where code meets hardware: hardware, firmware and software engineers bringing up, debugging, testing and flashing devices with a USB-C port. It fits on a desk, in an automated test bench and on a production line, where the product's own connector becomes its test port.

VBUS switch

Between computer and device, up to 20 V and 5 A, both directions, off at power-up.

Why Power-cycle the device from a script, and nothing reaches it until you ask.

Power meter

VBUS voltage, current both ways, power, charge and energy totals.

Why See what the device draws while it boots, and log it beside its console.

Guards in the firmware

Voltage, current and power limits that cut VBUS on their own. A cut stays latched until the mode is written again.

Why A script that hangs, or a PD charger that renegotiates to 20 V, can't leave a 5 V device on a voltage it didn't ask for. Design target, to be tested on rev B.

USB data switch

Connect or cut the device's D+/D− without unplugging.

Why The device disappears from the computer and re-enumerates on reconnect, without touching power.

SWD debug probe

CMSIS-DAP v2 on the plug's SBU pins. Works with probe-rs, OpenOCD and pyOCD.

Why A device with SWD on its sideband pins is flashed and debugged through the same cable that powers it.

Buses on SBU, either way round

UART, I2C, SWD, FDCAN, GPIO and ADC on the plug's SBU pins, at 1.8 or 3.3 V, with switchable pull-ups. UART, I2C and SWD swap their lines for either cable orientation.

Why The orientation is a setting, so a flipped cable is one command instead of re-plugging. Automatic TX/RX flip: {{ tbd | safe }}

Debug-accessory mode

Switchable pull-ups on the plug's CC lines, as a USB-C debug accessory.

Why The device opens its debug path (SWD, a UART) onto the SBU pins with a few comparators or ADC pins. No PD controller needed.

USB 2.0 Hi-Speed hub

The device under test and the on-board MCU on one cable. USB on the header and on the plug's SBU pins: {{ tbd | safe }}

Why The device's own USB keeps its 480 Mbit/s, and the dongle's control traffic takes little of it.

Header with a relay

4 protected GPIO with UART, I2C, SPI, FDCAN and ADC, 3.3 V out, switched VBUS and an isolated relay.

Why The relay presses a reset button, a boot strap or a power key on the device.

USB Power Delivery

A PD controller on each port, each a source or a sink. The PD stack in the firmware is planned for v1.0.

Why A small PD test tool: watch a contract, ask for another voltage, see the meter follow.

Safe by default

Every enable has a pull-down. Plug VBUS, the header's VBUS_SW and the relay are off at power-up, after a reset and while the MCU is halted.

Why The hardware holds the safe state, not the firmware.

One typed API

Rust library, Python wheel, the dg CLI and the {{ }} GUI over the same API.

Why Every tool sees the same functions and the same checks, and always matches the firmware.

The computer talks to the STM32G0B1 through port 1 of the hub. The MCU switches plug VBUS, cuts or connects the device's USB data, drives the sideband pins through a level translator and reads the power meter. Every enable has a pull-down: with the MCU in reset or halted, everything that could reach the device is off.

VBUS passes through a bidirectional load switch with a current shunt, so the computer can power the device or the device can feed the computer. Donguru itself runs from whichever side has VBUS (a diode OR into a 4.5 to 40 V buck converter), so it keeps working while plug VBUS is off, and a self-powered device can keep it alive.

SBU modes and the cable orientation

USB-C plugs go in either way round, and the device's CC pins can't tell which: a device has the same 5.1 kΩ pull-down on both. The orientation is a setting instead. FDCAN has a TX pin on SBU2 only, so it works in one orientation.

Button, switch and LEDs

A push button and a slide switch share one ADC input; the firmware decodes presses and their length, and the button can be given an action (power cycle, data reconnect). Holding the button at power-up starts the STM32 USB bootloader for a firmware update without a probe. A red and a green LED show state, and can blink to identify one dongle among several.

dg CLI

--format json or jsonl on every command for scripts, and exit codes that tell a refused request from a missing dongle. Completion for bash, zsh and fish. Planned for v1.0: dg uart, dg i2c, dg gpio.

probe-rs and friends

probe-rs uses the probe directly: probe-rs run --chip STM32G0B1CEUx firmware.elf with Donguru plugged in. OpenOCD and pyOCD find it like any other CMSIS-DAP probe.

Can a PD charger put 20 V on my 5 V board?

Not with the default mode. ForceOn keeps plug VBUS on only while the computer's VBUS stays at or below the limit, 5.5 V unless asked for more. The firmware checks the limits about every 15 ms: fast enough for a renegotiation, not a fuse. A short circuit is caught by the load switch itself. This is a design target, to be tested on rev B.

What happens when the firmware hangs or the MCU is halted?

Every enable has a pull-down. Plug VBUS, the header's VBUS_SW and the relay are off; the device's USB data stays connected to the hub. See At reset, halted or unpowered.

Does it work with probe-rs?

Yes. Donguru is a CMSIS-DAP v2 probe on the plug's SBU pins, so probe-rs, OpenOCD and pyOCD find it on USB like any other probe. The SWD clock goes up to 3 MHz bit-banged, and up to 4 MHz SPI-driven as an experiment.

What if the cable goes in the other way round?

The orientation is a setting, so a flipped cable is one command rather than re-plugging. UART, I2C and SWD swap their lines; FDCAN on SBU works in one orientation only.

Does my device need a PD controller for the debug path?

No. In debug-accessory mode Donguru pulls both CC pins of the device up. A device recognises that with a few comparators or ADC pins and switches its SWD or UART onto the SBU pins. Devices with a PD controller can be asked the same over PD, with a vendor message.

Can I use the UART as a plain serial port?

That is planned: the SBU UART as a standard USB serial port (CDC-ACM), which terminal programs open as /dev/ttyACM0 or COM3 with no Donguru software at all.

Does Donguru need its own power supply?

No. It runs from whichever side has VBUS, so it keeps working while plug VBUS is off, and a self-powered device can keep it alive.

How fast is the UART?

The baud rate goes up to 8 MBd, with 7 or 8 data bits and no flow control. Receiving runs sustained at 2 MBd (200 kB/s) with any data, without compression, measured on rev A. Above about 2 MBd the firmware compresses what it receives with LZ4 for hosts that support it, so the sustained rate depends on the data; those rates are {{ tbd | safe }}.

Can I write tests without the hardware?

Yes. The library carries a mock Donguru that follows the same rules as the firmware, for Rust tests today and for Python (Donguru.mock()) with the wheel.

How is its own firmware updated?

Holding the button at power-up starts the STM32 USB bootloader, for a firmware update without a probe.
